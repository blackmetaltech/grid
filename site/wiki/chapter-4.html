WO-10 Balance the Phases New look

Industrial Belt · Tanner Street, 19:00

Goal

Tanner Street was wired in a hurry for the festival and every house hangs on phase a of the transformer. Now the lights buzz at one end of the street and TVs spark at the other. Eighteen houses on a 230/400 V four-wire feeder: move each service drop to phase a, b or c. Keep the neutral current under 20 % of the biggest phase current and every far-pole voltage inside the supply band. Each house you re-connect is a crew visit.

Budget: $1,200.

Lamps: pass the job; spend at most $765; spend at most $680 and far-pole voltage within ± 5 % of 230 V, line losses ≤ 3 %.

If it fails

At 19:15 the neutral carried 153.5 A, but a 54.6 mm² neutral is rated 150 A. It overheated and burnt through.

That is the engine's own sentence for a first-try build that misses. Yours will name your numbers.

On the radio

  • ADA Year six. The festival crew had one transformer, three live wires, and no time. They hung every house on the same one.
  • TOBI Three phases are three waves 120° apart. Put the same load on each and the currents cancel in the neutral. Put it all on one and the neutral carries the lot.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Tap a house, then a phase. Every house starts on phase a, so the neutral is carrying all of their current.The neutral current is the phasor sum I_a + I_b + I_c. With all the load on one phase there is nothing to cancel it.
  2. Spread the houses over a, b and c until the neutral is under 20 % of the biggest phase current.Balance by apparent power (kW ÷ power factor), not by counting houses: a 4 kW air conditioner at pf 0.8 draws more amps than a 4 kW heater at pf 1.0.
  3. Press Run: the evening peak starts at 19:00 and runs to 23:00.A street has to hold through its peak, not just at one moment.
  4. Read the shift report: it shows the three phase currents and their sum in the neutral.A balanced street sends its currents round the three phases and almost none down the neutral.

WO-11 Star or Delta New look

Industrial Belt · Tanner Estate, 08:00

Goal

The Tanner estate runs 500 kW of motors at power factor 0.70 on a weak 400 V supply. Buy capacitors by the microfarad, wire them in star (Y) or delta, and lift the power factor to 0.95 without overshooting into a leading one. Then the 75 kW pump starts: the estate bus must not dip more than 10 % or the other motors trip. A can wired in delta sees the full 400 V, so mind its rating.

Budget: $22,000.

Lamps: pass the job; spend at most $17,028; spend at most $12,762 and pump start dips the bus ≤ 6 %.

If it fails

The pump started direct on line: 802 A against 134 A running. The estate bus dipped 11.5 % (the limit is 10 %) and the other motors tripped on undervoltage.

That is the engine's own sentence for a first-try build that misses. Yours will name your numbers.

On the radio

  • ADA The estate pays a penalty on every kVAr-hour it draws. Fix the power factor and the penalty goes, and so does a slice of the voltage drop.
  • TOBI Wire a bank in delta and each can sees 400 V, in star only 231. The same kVAr takes a third of the microfarads in delta. And that big pump starts at six times its running current if you let it.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Build a capacitor bank of about 346 kVAr. In star it takes three times the microfarads that delta does.Q_c = P (tan φ₁ − tan φ₂) is the reactive power to cancel. Per phase C = Q_c ÷ (3 ω V²) in delta, three times that in star.
  2. Fit a star-delta or a soft starter on the pump.The bus dips in proportion to the starting current. Star-delta starts with 58 % of the line voltage on each winding, so a third of the direct-on-line current.
  3. Press Run: the pump starts after three blocks.The dip only shows when the pump actually starts.
  4. Read the shift report: the bank, the power factor and the dip.Star and delta give the same kVAr from different microfarads; what changes is the voltage on each can.

WO-12 Over the Hill New look

Hydro Valley · Highfell Falls, 18:00

Goal

Highfell Falls hydro (two 100 MW units, inertia constant 3 s) sends 200 MW over two 300 km 220 kV circuits to the city. At 19:30 one circuit trips. One circuit can carry the power, so heat is not the limit: the angle is. Keep the plant in step through the swing. The AVR is still on its night set-point.

Budget: $1,200,000.

Lamps: pass the job; spend at most $480,000 and first swing peaks ≤ 70°; the third needs the same spend and first swing peaks ≤ 70°.

If it fails

At 19:30 one circuit tripped. The rotor angle swung past the unstable point of 95.9° (the plant was sending 200 MW with P_max after the trip of 2.01 pu) and slipped a pole 315 ms into the swing.

That is the engine's own sentence for a first-try build that misses. Yours will name your numbers.

On the radio

  • ADA Year seven. Highfell Falls has water and no neighbours. The city is 300 km away over the hill, and a long wire has a lot of reactance.
  • TOBI P = E V sin δ ÷ X. When a circuit goes, X doubles and the sine curve drops. The rotor has to find a new angle before it overshoots the top of the curve. Lose that race and the plant slips a pole.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Raise the AVR set-point: the night value is too low for 200 MW.More excitation means a higher internal emf E′, so a taller P-δ curve and more room to swing.
  2. Check P_max after the trip: it must stay clear of the 2.0 pu the plant sends.If P_max after the trip is below P_m there is no angle where the power balances and the rotor runs away.
  3. Press Run: one circuit trips at 19:30.The swing is only simulated when the trip happens, with the build you have then.
  4. Watch the rotor angle: it must turn back before the unstable point.The shaded area under P_m is the energy that speeds the rotor up; the area above the new curve is what slows it down. They must be equal before the unstable angle.

WO-13 Exam Bridge: Generator Phasors New look

Hydro Valley · Highfell Falls control room, 01:00

Goal

A cylindrical-rotor generator: V = 1.0 pu, I = 1.0 pu at power factor 0.8 lagging, X_s = 1.2 pu. Predict the internal emf E, the power angle δ and the maximum power P_max before you run. Then, at night, set the excitation so the unit absorbs 0.2 pu of reactive power at P = 0.6 pu while δ stays under 60°.

Lamps: pass the job; the card says what the second and third ask for.

If it fails

An answer outside the pass mark fails the job. The report then shows the working, so you can see where yours went off.

On the radio

  • NIA Exam tomorrow. Sir says the whole generator is one triangle: V, then jX_s I stuck on the end, and E is the long side.
  • ADA Draw I at 36.9° behind V, stand jX_s I up at right angles to it, and the closing side is E. Then P_max = E V ÷ X_s. At night the line charges up and the plant has to soak up Q: that is an under-excited machine.
Coach steps (hints that show how to build)
  1. Predict E: add jX_s I to V as phasors. Do not add the lengths.E² = V² + (X_s I)² + 2 V X_s I sin φ, because I lags V and jX_s I stands at right angles to I.
  2. Predict the power angle δ and P_max too.δ is the angle of E, and P_max = E V ÷ X_s is where sin δ = 1.
  3. Set the night excitation so the unit absorbs 0.2 pu of Q at P = 0.6 pu.P = E V sin δ ÷ X_s is held by the governor, so changing E moves δ and Q = (E V cos δ − V²) ÷ X_s together.
  4. Press Run: the exam is marked.Predictions are marked when you press Run, so make them first.
