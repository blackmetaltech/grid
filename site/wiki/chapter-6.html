WO-19 Fifty Hertz

The southern region, islanded · 16:00

The southern region is islanded for maintenance: 6.9 GW of evening load on twelve coal units and four hydro units, no sun. The shift starts at 16:00; at 20:00 the largest coal unit trips. Keep the frequency above the first load-shedding stage, with no town shed.

On the radio

  • ADA The region is cut off from the national grid for a day of maintenance. Whatever frequency we hold, we hold alone.
  • TOBI Twelve coal units and four hydro, six point nine gigawatts. The biggest unit trips at eight. Nobody knows why, only when.
  • VANCE Every unit I keep spinning is fuel on my bill. Every town the relays shed is a phone call on yours.

Steps

  1. Before you run: predict the RoCoF (rate of change of frequency: how fast the frequency falls, in Hz per second) at the instant of the trip.RoCoF = dP x f0 / (2 H S): the lost power over the stored energy of the machines that remain. Heavy spinning machines (large H x S) hold their speed, so more inertia slows the fall.
  2. Keep the spare coal unit (U17, "spare coal": a unit kept running and ready) spinning: it is the one unit that adds a whole unit of headroom. Batteries on frequency response help only the first seconds.Headroom must be at least the size of the biggest unit and it only works on units that follow frequency (droop). A battery answers in a fifth of a second but cannot last for hours.
  3. Run the shift to 21:30.The nadir is the lowest point of the swing. The first load-shedding stage is the line you must not cross.

WO-20 Sun Goes Down

The desert region · one clear day

One clear desert day: 3 GW of sun and nine coal units. At dusk the sun leaves faster than coal can follow. Before 14:00, add quick flexibility (peakers ordered on, a store, or the pumps moved to noon), then run the whole day.

On the radio

  • ADA Clear sky all day. By noon the sun carries the town. By seven it carries nothing.
  • TOBI A coal unit takes two and a half hours to get on the grid, and it cannot go below its technical minimum. That is the whole problem.
  • VANCE The farm pumps run at the evening peak because that is when the feeder is switched on. I would rather they ran at noon.

Steps

  1. First build, then run. 1: place peakers from the tray (or a store, or move the pumps to noon). 2: order each peaker on with its button in the control-room card. 3: run the day. Four 105 MW peakers and the pumps at noon is the cheapest pass.The sun takes the middle of the day from the coal and leaves in two hours while the town switches its lights on; a coal unit needs two and a half hours from order to grid. A peaker is a fast gas turbine, a store holds the noon surplus for dusk, and a pump moved to noon turns a dusk load into a noon one for free.
  2. Set a store to charge in the sun and discharge at dusk, or shift the pumps. Then run the day.Charge while the sun is surplus (it would be curtailed) and discharge when the net load climbs.
  3. Run the whole day.At its minimum a coal unit cannot hold the frequency, and at its maximum it cannot either: the noise shows at both ends of the day.

WO-21 Inertia Matters

The desert region · noon

Noon: 70 % of the power comes from inverters and the coal is off. A 500 MW unit trips. The rooftop inverters trip too if the frequency falls faster than 1 Hz/s over half a second, and that turns a small loss into a cascade. Hold the frequency.

On the radio

  • ADA Solar and rooftops carry seventy percent of the noon load. Inverters follow the grid, they do not hold it.
  • TOBI South Australia, 2016. The old rooftop settings tripped on rate of change, and a small loss became a state-wide blackout.
  • VANCE Inertia has a price now. Coal held hot, condensers, batteries that behave like machines: pick the cheapest that keeps the lights on.

Steps

  1. Before you run: predict the RoCoF at the instant of the trip, in Hz per second, in the box on the control-room card.RoCoF = dP x f0 / (2 H S), with S and H of the machines that are left after the trip.
  2. Bring inertia and fast response back: hold a coal unit hot, add condensers, grid-forming batteries, or fit the rooftops with ride-through.Machines store energy in their rotors. A grid-forming battery acts like a machine for a few seconds. Ride-through only stops the rooftops from adding to the loss.
  3. Run the shift to 14:30.The nadir has to stay above 49.4 Hz; the third lamp asks for a shallower swing.

WO-22 Exam Bridge: Droop & RoCoF

The exam hall · two machines and a 100 MW step

Three textbook questions, then a task. Predict the numbers, set the droops of two machines so that they share a 100 MW step 60 to 40, and see whether the engine agrees with the book.

On the radio

  • ADA Three questions from the textbook. Write the answers down before the machine runs.
  • TOBI Droop is a share rule: a machine takes its rating over its droop. Halve the droop and it takes twice the step.
  • VANCE G1 is 500 MVA, G2 300 MVA. The operator wants them sharing 60 to 40. They start at the textbook droops.

Steps

  1. Answer the three questions: examA (RoCoF, Hz/s), examB (df, Hz) and examC (G1 share, MW) before you run.A prediction made first is worth more than a result read afterwards. The stars are for the answers within one percent.
  2. Set the droops of G1 and G2 so that they share the 100 MW step 60 to 40.A machine takes its share of the step in proportion to S/R: rating over droop.
  3. Run to the trip of G3, the 100 MW step.The window plays the step on the frequency dial: the machines answer in proportion to S/R and the load helps a little.
